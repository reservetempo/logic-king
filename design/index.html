The Architect

The Book of Servants

Play the prototype

You are the Architect. Every flame on these roads is yours to set, every servant’s fate yours to decide, and of them you build a machine. Signals are torchbearers, monks who walk the roads between gates, each carrying a single torch: a lit torch is 1, a dark one is 0. A gate receives the torches that arrive and sends one onward, lit or dark, as its keeper judges.

  • Monks carry A monk never changes a torch on the road. He only walks.
  • Priests combine A priest weighs two torches and sends one on.
  • High priests negate A high priest overturns what reaches him. The greater high priests keep a priest at their side and overturn his verdict.

Every silhouette echoes its gate symbol, and every high priest wears a small ring (a collar, a brooch, a clasp): the bubble of negation.

Monks carry, priests weigh, high priests overturn.

The signal

The Bearer

Monk

Class
Monk · carries
Carries
One torch, lit (1) or dark (0)

Personality

Silent, obedient and without question. A bearer never kindles or quenches a torch on the road: he carries what he is given to the next gate and kneels there until he is sent on. He keeps vigil in the queues, murmurs the hours under his breath, and never asks what the Architect is building.

Look

Small and hooded, with short legs and a slow, bowed walk so a column reads at a glance. A rough dark habit, a rope girdle, bare feet grey with ash. The torch rides high on a long pole so its state shows from across the map: lit, it throws a warm halo; dark, it is a charred wick with a thread of smoke. The color of hood or stole (oxblood, deep blue, bone) tells one bearer from another.

NOT

High priest

High priest · negates
Torches

The rule

Bring me fire and I will put it out. Bring me ashes and I will kindle them. Nothing leaves my altar as it came: whatever you carry in, you carry the opposite out.

Personality

Grave, unbending and wholly certain. He answers no before the question is finished, and yes to any who come seeking no. He does not scheme and he does not relent, so the harshest judge in all the world is also the one most surely relied upon. The bearers bow lowest at his gate.

Look

Tall and gaunt, his black cope falling from the shoulders in a hard triangle, with a ring of bone at the throat: the NOT symbol worn as vestment. A hollow face, ash on the brow, a long snuffer in one hand and a flint in the other. Colors: black, rubric-red lining, tarnished gold.

Personality

Patient, exacting and slow to judge. He will not be hurried, and half a testimony does not move him; he weighs both torches twice before he gives his verdict. Disputes with the priest of OR through every night vigil, and never yields.

Look

Broad and solid: a heavy black chasuble swells round over his back and hangs flat in front, giving him the flat-fronted, rounded D of the AND symbol. A shaven head, a stole of rubric red, a pair of scales at his girdle. Colors: black, oxblood, tarnished gold.

Personality

Merciful, quick to grant and slow to refuse: a single flame is reason enough for him to give light. He keeps the night office in a low chant and gives away his bread. Thinks the priest of AND hard of heart, and tells him so, gently.

Look

Tall and spare; a pointed cowl and a chasuble that sweeps back in a curve give him the curved, pointed shield of the OR symbol. Ash-grey vestments, a deep-blue stole, and a small lamp at his girdle that he never lets go out. Colors: ash grey, deep blue, bone, candle yellow.

NAND

High priest

Class
High priest · negates, with the AND priest at his side
Torches
Two in, one out

The rule

My priest weighs the torches as AND was taught to weigh them, and then I overturn his judgment. Two lit torches leave dark. Anything less leaves lit.

Truth table

0 dark0 dark1 lit

Personality

Distrustful of consensus: when every torch agrees he suspects a false witness and overturns the verdict. In every other case he is lenient, almost merciful, so most torches leave his altar lit. He holds that his order alone could build the whole machine without any other gate, and, gravely, he is right.

Look

Heavy and broad, in a rounded cope that repeats the AND priest beside him, with a large ring brooch of tarnished gold at the breast: the bubble of negation. The AND priest stands at his elbow with scales in hand, so the sprite is a pair, high priest in front and priest half a step behind. Colors: oxblood, tarnished gold, black.

Two in, one out

The rule

My priest would give light if either torch were burning. I forbid it. The torch that leaves my altar shall burn only when both arrive dark.

Personality

Cold, still and austere, a keeper of the long night. He trusts darkness and receives any flame as an offense, so only two dark torches find favor with him. Like NAND he could build the whole machine with his order alone; unlike NAND, he would never say so aloud.

Look

Tall and motionless, in a deep-blue cope whose curved, pointed hem sweeps back in the shield of OR, with a ring clasp of bone at the collar. The OR priest walks at his side carrying a lamp the high priest keeps dark. Colors: deep blue, bone, ash grey, one rubric-red stole.

The world

The Architect’s View

The machine, seen from above

Hour
Night, and only night
Gaze
Fixed, from the south, 45° down
Light
Cold moon, warm fire

The gaze

You look down on your works from the south, at forty-five degrees, and your gaze does not turn. The road comes in at the top of the world and goes out at the bottom, and every servant on it walks beneath your eye.

The diorama

The land is a dark toy laid out on a table, as in the remake of Link’s Awakening: chunky bevelled tiles set a little apart so the dark shows between them, soft rounded forms, and servants made as glossy painted pieces, like chessmen lifted from their box. The top and bottom of the view fall soft, as if seen too near, with tilt-shift blur.

The night

But this toy has been left out after dark, in the gloom of Twilight Princess. The sky is a deep blue-black. A low mist lies over the grass, and the greens and earths are drained near to grey. The moon gives every edge a cold rim and warms nothing. The torches and fires are the only warm light in the world, and they bloom.

The places

  • The road Packed earth, worn by bare feet, running from the top of the screen to the bottom.
  • The chapels Each gate is a small dark chapel or shrine beside the road, with one arch and one low lamp. Its priest stands at the door and receives the torches.
  • The beacon At the road’s end stands a stone tower with an iron bowl, the first lamp of the Architect’s machine. A lit torch that reaches it wakes it.
